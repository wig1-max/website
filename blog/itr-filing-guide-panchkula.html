Why File Your ITR — Even If You Owe Zero Tax?

Every year, thousands of Panchkula residents skip filing their Income Tax Return because they believe it is only necessary when you owe money to the government. This is one of the biggest financial misconceptions. Filing your ITR is not just about paying taxes — it is a powerful financial document that opens doors in many areas of your life.

Here are three compelling reasons why you should file your ITR every year, even if your tax liability is zero:

  • Loan applications require ITR: Whether you are applying for a home loan, car loan, personal loan, or even a credit card with a high limit, banks and NBFCs in Panchkula and across India mandatorily ask for ITR receipts of the last 2-3 years. Without filed returns, your application is likely to be rejected or delayed — regardless of how strong your income is.
  • Visa processing depends on ITR: Planning a trip to the US, UK, Canada, Europe, or Australia? Embassies and consulates routinely ask for ITR acknowledgments as proof of your financial standing and ties to India. If you are a Panchkula resident planning international travel, filed ITRs are practically non-negotiable for visa approval.
  • Carry forward losses for future benefit: If you have incurred capital losses from stocks, mutual funds, or property sales during the financial year, you can only carry them forward to offset future gains if you file your ITR before the due date. Miss the deadline, and those losses vanish forever — a costly mistake many taxpayers make unknowingly.

Beyond these three reasons, many Panchkula residents actually miss out on legitimate tax refunds by not filing. If your employer has deducted TDS but your actual tax liability is lower (or zero), the only way to get that money back is by filing your ITR. We regularly help clients in Panchkula, Chandigarh, and Mohali recover refunds they did not even know they were entitled to.

Which ITR Form Should You Use?

One of the most common sources of confusion during tax season is choosing the correct ITR form. Filing with the wrong form can lead to your return being treated as defective by the Income Tax Department, resulting in notices and delays. Here is a straightforward breakdown of the six main ITR forms and who should use each one:

ITR Form Also Known As Who Should Use It
ITR-1 Sahaj Salaried individuals with total income up to ₹50 lakh from salary, one house property, and other sources (interest, etc.)
ITR-2 — Individuals and HUFs with capital gains, multiple house properties, foreign income, or income above ₹50 lakh
ITR-3 — Individuals and HUFs with income from business or profession (not opting for presumptive taxation)
ITR-4 Sugam Individuals, HUFs, and firms with presumptive income under Sections 44AD, 44ADA, or 44AE
ITR-5 — Partnership firms, LLPs, AOPs, BOIs, and similar entities (not applicable to individuals)
ITR-6 — Companies other than those claiming exemption under Section 11 (charitable/religious trusts)

Documents You Need Before Filing

Before you sit down to file your ITR (or hand it off to a professional), make sure you have the following documents ready. Having everything organized beforehand can save you hours of back-and-forth and ensure nothing gets missed:

  • PAN Card: Your Permanent Account Number is the primary identifier for all tax filings in India.
  • Aadhaar Card (linked to PAN): PAN-Aadhaar linking is mandatory. If yours is not linked yet, get it done before filing or your return may not be processed.
  • Form 16 from employer: This is the TDS certificate your employer provides, showing your salary details and tax deducted at source. Most employers in Panchkula issue this by June.
  • Form 16A / 16B / 16C: TDS certificates for income other than salary — such as interest on fixed deposits (16A), property sale TDS (16B), or rent TDS (16C).
  • Bank statements (all accounts): Collect statements for every bank account you hold, including savings, current, and fixed deposit accounts. The interest earned is taxable and must be reported.
  • Investment proofs (Section 80C, 80D, etc.): This includes PPF receipts, ELSS statements, life insurance premium receipts, health insurance premium receipts, NPS contribution receipts, tuition fee receipts, and home loan principal repayment certificates.
  • Capital gains statements: If you have sold mutual funds, shares, property, or other capital assets during the year, gather your buy and sell transaction records, CAMS/KFintech statements, and broker contract notes.
  • Property details: If you own or co-own any property, keep the address, loan details, rent received (if applicable), and municipal tax receipts handy.
  • Previous year ITR acknowledgment: Having your prior year return helps cross-verify carried-forward losses, brought-forward depreciation, and previous tax credits.

Pro Tip: Create a dedicated folder on your phone or computer called "ITR 2024-25" and scan or photograph all these documents as you receive them throughout the year. When filing season arrives, you will have everything in one place instead of scrambling at the last minute. Many of our Panchkula clients use this simple habit and it saves them significant stress every year.

Step-by-Step ITR Filing Process

Whether you are filing for the first time or the tenth, here is the complete step-by-step process for filing your Income Tax Return for FY 2024-25:

  1. Step 1: Gather all documents. Collect every document listed in the section above. Cross-check your Form 26AS (available on the income tax portal) with your Form 16 and bank TDS certificates to make sure all deductions are reflected correctly.
  2. Step 2: Choose the correct ITR form. Refer to the table above to pick the right form for your income type and sources. Filing the wrong form is one of the top reasons for defective return notices.
  3. Step 3: Login to the Income Tax portal. Go to incometax.gov.in and log in with your PAN and password. If you are a first-time filer, you will need to register first using your PAN, Aadhaar, and a valid mobile number.
  4. Step 4: Fill in income details. Enter your salary income, house property income, capital gains, business income, and income from other sources as applicable. The portal pre-fills some data from your Form 26AS and AIS (Annual Information Statement) — verify these carefully.
  5. Step 5: Claim deductions and exemptions. Enter all eligible deductions under Section 80C (up to ₹1.5 lakh), 80D (health insurance), 80E (education loan interest), 80G (donations), HRA exemption, LTA, and any other applicable sections. If you are opting for the new tax regime, most of these deductions will not apply, but standard deduction of ₹75,000 is available.
  6. Step 6: Verify the tax computation. Review the auto-calculated tax liability carefully. Check if the TDS already deducted covers your full liability. If there is additional tax to pay, you will need to pay it via challan before submitting. If you have paid excess tax, you will see a refund due.
  7. Step 7: Submit and e-verify. After reviewing everything, submit your return. You must then e-verify it within 30 days to complete the process. The easiest and fastest method is Aadhaar OTP verification — an OTP is sent to the mobile number linked with your Aadhaar, and you simply enter it on the portal. Other methods include net banking, bank account EVC, and Demat account EVC.

Important Deadlines for FY 2024-25

Missing a tax deadline does not just mean a penalty — it can also mean losing the ability to carry forward losses, paying interest on outstanding tax, and triggering scrutiny. Here are the critical dates you need to mark on your calendar for the FY 2024-25 assessment year:

Category Deadline Notes
Individual (non-audit) July 31, 2025 Salaried individuals, freelancers, and small taxpayers not requiring audit
Audit cases October 31, 2025 Businesses and professionals whose accounts are subject to tax audit
Revised return December 31, 2025 If you discover an error in your original filing, you can file a revised return by this date
Belated return December 31, 2025 If you miss the original deadline, you can still file — but with a penalty

Late filing penalty: If your total income is below ₹5 lakh, the penalty is ₹1,000. For income above ₹5 lakh, the penalty is ₹5,000. On top of this, you will also pay interest at 1% per month under Section 234A on any tax due from the original deadline until the date of filing. The bottom line: file on time, every time.

Common ITR Filing Mistakes to Avoid

After handling hundreds of ITR filings for clients across Panchkula, Chandigarh, and Mohali, we have seen the same costly mistakes repeated year after year. Here are the five most common ones — and how to avoid them:

  1. Not reporting all bank accounts: The Income Tax Department has access to your financial data through AIS (Annual Information Statement). If you hold multiple bank accounts and fail to disclose all of them in your ITR, it raises red flags. Even dormant accounts with minimal interest must be reported.
  2. Forgetting to declare fixed deposit interest: Many people assume that TDS deducted on FD interest means they do not need to report it. Wrong. FD interest is fully taxable and must be declared under "Income from Other Sources," even if TDS has been deducted. The TDS is just an advance payment — you still need to report the full interest amount.
  3. Missing capital gains from mutual fund redemptions: Every time you redeem or switch mutual fund units, it triggers a capital gains event. Many Panchkula investors forget to include these gains (or losses) in their return. Even Systematic Transfer Plans (STPs) create taxable events for each transfer.
  4. Not claiming all eligible deductions: We regularly find that clients have missed deductions they are fully entitled to — health insurance premiums for parents under Section 80D, education loan interest under Section 80E, donations under Section 80G, or even the ₹50,000 NPS deduction under Section 80CCD(1B). Every missed deduction is money left on the table.
  5. Filing the wrong ITR form: Using ITR-1 when you should be using ITR-2 (for example, if you have capital gains) leads to the return being marked as defective. You will then need to refile within the time limit given, adding stress and potential delays to your refund.

These mistakes can trigger an income tax notice, lead to penalties, or cause you to pay more tax than necessary. Professional filing eliminates these risks entirely.
