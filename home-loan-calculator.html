Understanding Home Loans in India

How is Home Loan EMI Calculated?

The EMI (Equated Monthly Installment) is calculated using the standard reducing balance formula:

EMI = P × r × (1+r)n / ((1+r)n − 1)

Where P = Principal loan amount, r = Monthly interest rate (annual rate / 12 / 100), and n = Total number of monthly installments (years × 12).

For example, a ₹50,00,000 loan at 8.5% for 20 years gives a monthly EMI of approximately ₹43,391. Over the full tenure, you would pay around ₹54,14,000 as interest — more than the original loan amount!

Home Loan Eligibility Factors

Banks consider several factors when determining your home loan eligibility:

  • Income: Your net monthly income is the primary factor. Banks typically allow EMI up to 40–50% of net income (called FOIR — Fixed Obligations to Income Ratio).
  • Age: Younger applicants get longer tenures, which increases eligible loan amounts.
  • Credit Score: A CIBIL score of 750+ gets you the best rates and higher loan amounts.
  • Existing Loans: Any existing EMIs reduce your eligible amount as they eat into your FOIR.
  • Employment Stability: Salaried individuals with 2+ years of experience and stable employment history are preferred.
  • Property Value: Banks fund up to 75–90% of the property value (LTV ratio), depending on loan amount.

Tax Benefits on Home Loans

Home loans offer significant tax benefits under the Income Tax Act:

  • Section 24(b): Deduction up to ₹2,00,000 per year on interest paid for a self-occupied property. For let-out properties, the entire interest is deductible.
  • Section 80C: Deduction up to ₹1,50,000 per year on principal repayment (part of the ₹1.5L overall 80C limit).
  • Section 80EEA: Additional deduction of up to ₹1,50,000 on interest for first-time homebuyers (subject to stamp duty value limits and conditions).
  • Joint Loan Benefit: If you take a joint home loan with your spouse, both can independently claim the above deductions, effectively doubling the tax benefit.

Tips for Getting Your Home Loan Approved

  • Maintain a high CIBIL score: Pay all existing EMIs and credit card bills on time. A score above 750 significantly improves your chances.
  • Keep documentation ready: Salary slips, bank statements (6 months), Form 16, ITR (2–3 years for self-employed), and property documents.
  • Reduce existing debt: Close small loans and reduce credit card outstanding before applying.
  • Choose the right tenure: A longer tenure reduces EMI and improves eligibility, but increases total interest.
  • Add a co-applicant: A working spouse as co-applicant increases combined income and loan eligibility.
  • Compare across banks: Interest rates and processing fees vary. Compare at least 3–4 lenders before deciding.

Under Section 24(b), you can claim up to ₹2,00,000 deduction on interest paid for a self-occupied property. Under Section 80C, you can claim up to ₹1,50,000 deduction on principal repayment. First-time buyers may claim an additional ₹1,50,000 under Section 80EEA (subject to conditions).

While longer tenures (20–30 years) reduce your EMI, they significantly increase total interest paid. A 15–20 year tenure is generally considered optimal as it balances affordable EMIs with reasonable total interest outgo. Always try to prepay when possible to reduce your interest burden.

In India, most home loans are on floating rates linked to the repo rate. Floating rates are generally 1–2% lower than fixed rates and benefit you when rates fall. Fixed rates provide payment certainty but are usually higher. For long-tenure loans, a floating rate is generally recommended as rates tend to average out over time.
